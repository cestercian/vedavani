Privacy Policy

VedaVani — iPhone and iPad

Last updated 18 November 2026

VedaVani is a quiet companion to Hindu scripture — the Bhagavad Gita, the Upanishads, the Vedas and the epics. It is built to work without an account, and to keep what you write on your own device unless you explicitly choose otherwise.

The short version

  • Everything you write is stored on your device by default.
  • Signing in is optional. It exists only so your words can be backed up and restored if you lose or replace your phone.
  • We show no ads, use no analytics, and do no tracking.
  • Guided answers are optional. Only if you turn them on is a question you ask sent off your phone — to our server, to Anthropic so that AI can choose the passage and write the answer, and the answer to OpenAI so it can be read aloud.
  • We never sell or share your data, and we do not use it to train models.

What we store, and when

Without an account (default). Your reflections, your Ask history, your name, and your reminder time are stored only on your device, in the app's private storage. Nothing leaves your phone. Deleting the app deletes this data.

With an account (optional). If you choose Sign in with Apple, we store the following on servers operated by Supabase (hosted in Tokyo, Japan) so it can be restored to your devices:

DataWhy
A user ID (a random identifier from Apple)To keep your data yours
Your email addressProvided by Apple at sign-in; identifies your account. If you chose Apple's "Hide My Email", we only ever see the relay address
Your first nameProvided by Apple at sign-in, only to greet you by name
Your reflections (written in earlier versions of the app)So they can be restored
Your Ask questions and the verses returnedSo they can be restored

Your data is protected by row-level security: your account can read and write only its own rows.

Microphone and speech

When you tap to ask aloud, the app records audio only while you are speaking, and only to turn it into text. That audio is never sent to our servers and is never stored. Speech-to-text is performed by Apple's speech recognition on your device; depending on your device, language, and settings, Apple may process some audio on its own servers, which is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy. You can use the app entirely without the microphone by typing your question instead.

Guided answers (optional)

The first time you ask a question, the app asks whether to use guided answers. If you say no, everything stays on your phone: the library is searched on the device and the closest passage is chosen there.

If you say yes, each question you ask is sent to our server (a Supabase Edge Function, hosted in Tokyo, Japan) together with:

DataWhy
The passages your phone found for the questionSo the best one can be chosen. These are public scripture texts
Your phone's language settingSo the answer is written and spoken in your language
Up to 12 short memory notes (see below)So the answer understands what you are going through
The app versionTo keep the reply compatible with your app
A random install identifier, created by the appTo apply a daily fair-use limit. It is not derived from your device or from you; deleting the app resets it
Your account token, if you are signed inTo apply the limit to your account instead

Our server forwards the question, your language, your notes and the passages to Anthropic's Claude API, which chooses one passage, retells it in simple words and writes a short reflection. Anthropic processes this for us under its commercial terms and its Privacy Policy; it does not use it to train its models. Our server then sends the text of that answer — the retelling and the reflection, not your question — to OpenAI's text-to-speech API so it can be read aloud to you. OpenAI processes it for us under its API terms and its Privacy Policy; it does not use data sent through its API to train its models. The scripture you see always comes from the library inside the app — AI only chooses which passage to show, and anything AI writes is labelled as such.

With guided answers on, the day's verse on the Today page is also sent to Claude once so it can be retold in simple words. It is public scripture text, sent with your language and nothing about you.

Memory notes

With guided answers on, Claude may write a short note after an answer — for example "Preparing for a job interview after a long gap" — so that later answers understand what you are going through. Notes are stored only on your device (they are not part of your backup) and the newest are sent with each guided question. You can read every note in Settings → Memory, delete any one of them, or forget them all. Start over and Delete all data also clear them.

We do not store your question on our server, and our logs record only counts and timings. To count the daily limit we keep the install identifier or account ID and a keyed hash of your network address (the key is secret and changes daily, so it can't be turned back into the address), each with a number of requests for the day — never the question. These counts are deleted automatically after a day, and an account's counts when the account is deleted. Like any web service, our hosting provider's infrastructure may log network addresses briefly for security. Questions you ask are kept in your Ask history on your device (and in your backup, if you are signed in), as described above.

You can turn guided answers off at any time in Settings.

Horoscope (optional)

If you add your birth details for the daily rashiphal — date of birth, and optionally your birth time and place — they are stored only on your device. They are not part of your backup, are not sent to our server, and are cleared by Start over and Delete all data. Your chart and readings are computed on the device.

When you search for your birth place, the letters you type are sent to Apple's map service to suggest places; this is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Palm reading (optional)

If you use palm reading, the camera feed and the photo you take — or a photo you choose from your library — are analysed on your device. VedaVani never saves the photo, adds it to your backup, or sends it to us or anyone else: it is held in memory while your reading is open and discarded when you start another reading or the app quits. The app only accesses the camera while the palm reading camera is open, and only photos you pick yourself.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily verse reminder, the reminder is scheduled locally on your device — including, if you added birth details, the day's rashiphal line. No notification data is sent to us or through a server.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or telemetry SDKs.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no data brokers.
  • No third-party services other than these, each acting on our behalf and not for its own purposes: Supabase, which stores your backup and runs our server; Anthropic, which chooses passages and writes guided answers if you turn them on; OpenAI, which reads guided answers aloud if you turn them on (it receives only the answer's text); and RevenueCat, which confirms Pro purchases with Apple (it receives a purchase identifier, and your account ID if you are signed in, but no questions or reflections).

Deleting your data

Both options are in the app, under the gear icon:

  • Delete all data (when signed out) removes everything VedaVani keeps on this device: your reflections, Ask history, memory notes, birth details, name and reminder.
  • Delete account and data (when signed in) does the same and also permanently deletes your backup and your account. This cannot be undone.

You may also simply delete the app, which removes everything stored on the device. If you had an account, use Delete account and data first.

Children

VedaVani is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Scripture

Every English translation in the library is in the public domain; the app's Sources page credits each translator. Sanskrit text, where shown, comes from openly licensed digital editions, also credited there.
